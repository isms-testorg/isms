# Organisational Context and Interested Parties

This document is part of the information security management system of dev-test. It is maintained under version control; the signed git history and the release packs are its change record.

## 1. Purpose

This document records the internal and external context relevant to dev-test's fictional demonstration ISMS and identifies the interested parties whose requirements influence information security. It is used when setting objectives, assessing risks, selecting controls, and reviewing changes.

## 2. Scope

dev-test is a small, distributed software engineering organization with employees and contractors working from home offices and other approved locations. Its main activities are software development, source-code management, release management, customer-data processing, and operation of supporting SaaS and cloud services. The organization depends on GitHub, CI/CD automation, an identity provider, endpoint devices, cloud hosting, secret storage, backups, and specialist suppliers. Relevant external factors include cyber threats, cloud concentration, changing privacy expectations, customer assurance demands, and legal or contractual retention duties.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities

The managing director, CTO, engineering lead, IT lead, and ISMS owner maintain awareness of context and interested-party requirements within their areas. The ISMS owner maintains the interested-parties register and coordinates reviews. Service and information owners identify dependencies and changes; employees, contractors, customers, suppliers, and authorities provide information through the channels defined for their relationship with dev-test.

## 4. Requirements

Customers require protection of confidential information, reliable service, secure development, incident communication, and fulfillment of agreed privacy and retention terms. Employees and contractors require clear responsibilities, appropriate access, training, and safe reporting channels. Regulators and supervisory authorities require lawful processing, cooperation, and records. Suppliers and cloud providers must meet agreed security, availability, privacy, and notification commitments. Owners and management require risk visibility, resource information, and evidence suitable for decisions. These requirements are monitored through contracts, supplier reviews, risk and objective reviews, incident handling, and compliance checks.

## 5. Exceptions

Context changes and new interested parties are assessed when services, processing activities, suppliers, locations, personnel arrangements, or legal and contractual requirements change. Conflicting requirements are escalated to the ISMS owner and responsible management role for a documented risk and compliance decision. No interested-party requirement authorizes access beyond business need or overrides privacy and security obligations.

## 6. Monitoring and compliance

The ISMS owner reviews the interested-parties register at least annually and after material change. Relevant requirements are mapped into risks, objectives, policies, procedures, contracts, and monitoring activities. Overdue reviews, unresolved conflicts, and material supplier or regulatory changes are reported to management. This is fictional demonstration content and does not evidence actual communications, compliance, or certification.

## 7. References

References include ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 4.1, 4.2, 6.1, and 9.3; the interested-parties register; risk and objectives registers; supplier-management and incident procedures; applicable GDPR/DSGVO requirements; customer contracts; and supplier agreements.
